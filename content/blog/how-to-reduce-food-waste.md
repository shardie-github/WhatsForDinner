# How to Reduce Food Waste: Use What You Bought

Food waste is a massive problem. In the US alone, we waste **40% of food produced**, costing families an average of **$1,500 per year**. But what if you could reduce food waste simply by using ingredients you already have?

That's where pantry-first meal planning comes in. Instead of buying new ingredients for recipes, you use what you already have—reducing waste and saving money.

## The Food Waste Problem

### Statistics
- **40%** of food produced in the US is wasted
- **$1,500** per year wasted per average family
- **31%** of food waste happens at home
- **21%** of food waste is fruits and vegetables

### Why We Waste Food
1. **Buying Too Much**: Impulse purchases, bulk buying
2. **Not Planning**: Buying without meal planning
3. **Expiration Dates**: Throwing away food before it expires
4. **Leftovers**: Not using leftovers creatively
5. **Recipe Shopping**: Buying ingredients for one recipe, not using them again

## The Solution: Pantry-First Meal Planning

**Pantry-first meal planning** flips the script. Instead of:
1. Find recipe → Buy ingredients → Cook → Waste unused ingredients

You do:
1. Check pantry → Use ingredients → Cook → Reduce waste

### Benefits
- **Reduce Food Waste**: Use ingredients before they expire
- **Save Money**: Use what you have, buy less
- **Save Time**: No shopping required
- **Reduce Stress**: No meal planning needed

## How to Reduce Food Waste: 10 Tips

### 1. Start with What You Have
Before shopping, check your pantry, fridge, and freezer. Use ingredients you already have before buying new ones.

**Tip**: Use What's for Dinner? to generate recipes from ingredients you already have.

### 2. Plan Meals Around Expiring Items
Check expiration dates weekly. Plan meals around items expiring soon.

**Example**: Chicken expires in 2 days? Generate recipes with chicken tonight.

### 3. Use Leftovers Creatively
Don't let leftovers go to waste. Transform them into new meals.

**Ideas**:
- Leftover chicken → Chicken salad, chicken soup, chicken tacos
- Leftover rice → Fried rice, rice pudding, rice bowls
- Leftover vegetables → Stir-fry, soup, frittata

### 4. Buy Only What You Need
Avoid bulk buying unless you'll use everything. Buy ingredients you'll use within a week.

**Tip**: Plan meals before shopping, not after.

### 5. Store Food Properly
Proper storage extends food life and reduces waste.

**Tips**:
- Store fruits and vegetables separately
- Use airtight containers
- Freeze leftovers immediately
- Label and date everything

### 6. Use "Ugly" Produce
Don't throw away produce that looks imperfect. It's still perfectly good.

**Tip**: Use imperfect produce in soups, stews, and smoothies.

### 7. Freeze Before Expiration
Freeze ingredients before they expire. You can use them later.

**Examples**:
- Freeze bread before it goes stale
- Freeze fruits before they spoil
- Freeze vegetables before they wilt

### 8. Use Everything (Stems, Leaves, etc.)
Don't waste parts of vegetables. Use stems, leaves, and peels.

**Examples**:
- Broccoli stems → Slice and sauté
- Carrot tops → Use in pesto
- Potato peels → Roast for chips

### 9. Batch Cooking
Cook in batches and use ingredients efficiently.

**Tip**: Make large batches of soup, stew, or casseroles using multiple ingredients.

### 10. Track What You Waste
Keep a food waste journal. Track what you throw away and why.

**Insight**: Identify patterns and reduce waste sources.
