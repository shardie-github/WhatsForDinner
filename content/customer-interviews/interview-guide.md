# Customer Interview Guide: What's for Dinner?

**Purpose**: Systematic approach to conducting user interviews for product validation and market fit  
**Duration**: 45-60 minutes per interview  
**Target**: 10-20 interviews per phase

---

## Pre-Interview Preparation

### Selecting Participants

**Ideal Participants:**
- **Primary ICP**: Busy families (28-45 years old, children 2-12)
- **Secondary ICP**: Diet-restricted consumers (keto, vegan, allergies)
- **Tertiary ICP**: Meal prep enthusiasts

**Recruitment Sources:**
- Existing users (if any)
- Social media (Facebook groups, Reddit communities)
- Parenting forums
- Meal prep communities
- Paid user testing platforms (UserTesting.com, Respondent.io)

**Compensation:**
- Free premium account (3 months)
- $25 gift card
- Early access to features

### Interview Setup

**Tools Needed:**
- Video conferencing (Zoom, Google Meet)
- Screen recording (with permission)
- Note-taking template (provided)
- Demo environment ready
- Product demo link

**Scheduling:**
- Send calendar invite with link
- Email reminder 24 hours before
- Send 15-minute reminder
- Include interview guide preview

## Interview Structure

### Phase 1: Introduction (5 minutes)

**Goals:**
- Build rapport
- Set expectations
- Get consent for recording

**Script:**
```
"Hi [Name], thanks for taking the time today! I'm [Your Name], and I'm working on What's for Dinner, a meal planning app.

Today is about learning from you—there are no right or wrong answers. I want to understand how you currently plan meals and what challenges you face.

Is it okay if I record this conversation? It's just for our internal notes, and we won't share it publicly.

This will take about 45 minutes. Sound good?"
```

**Questions:**
1. "Tell me a bit about yourself—what do you do, and how many people are in your household?"
2. "How do you currently plan meals for your family?"

---

### Phase 2: Problem Discovery (15 minutes)

**Goals:**
- Understand current meal planning process
- Identify pain points
- Understand emotional drivers

**Questions:**

#### Current Process
1. "Walk me through how you decide what to cook for dinner tonight."
   - **Probe**: "How long does this take? What steps are involved?"
   - **Probe**: "Where do you get recipe ideas from?"

2. "Tell me about the last time you felt stressed about meal planning."
   - **Probe**: "What made it stressful?"
   - **Probe**: "How did you solve it?"

3. "How do you use your pantry when meal planning?"
   - **Probe**: "Do you check what you have first, or decide what to make then shop?"
   - **Probe**: "Do you ever waste ingredients because you forgot you had them?"

#### Pain Points
4. "What's the most frustrating part of meal planning?"
   - **Probe**: "Can you give me a specific example?"
   - **Probe**: "How often does this happen?"

5. "Have you tried any meal planning apps or tools?"
   - **Probe**: "Which ones? What did you like/dislike?"
   - **Probe**: "Why did you stop using them?"

6. "How do you handle dietary restrictions or preferences?"
   - **Probe**: "Is this a challenge when meal planning?"
   - **Probe**: "What solutions have you tried?"

---

### Phase 3: Solution Validation (20 minutes)

**Goals:**
- Test core value proposition
- Understand feature priorities
- Validate differentiation

**Demo Script:**

1. **Show Homepage**
   ```
   "This is What's for Dinner. The core idea is: you tell us what's in your pantry, and we suggest recipes in 30 seconds."
   ```

2. **Live Demo**
   - Walk through adding pantry items
   - Generate recipes
   - Show recipe card
   - Show how to save favorites

**Questions During Demo:**

1. **Value Prop Test**
   - "Does this solve the problem we discussed earlier?"
   - "What would make you use this instead of your current method?"
   - "What's missing?"

2. **Feature Priorities**
   - "If you could add one feature, what would it be?"
   - "Which of these would be most valuable:
     - Weekly meal planning
     - Grocery list generation
     - Nutrition tracking
     - Recipe sharing
     - Cost calculator"

3. **Differentiation**
   - "How is this different from [competitor they mentioned]?"
   - "What would make you switch from your current method?"

4. **Pricing**
   - "Would you pay for this? How much?"
   - "What would make it worth paying for?"
   - "Would you prefer free with ads, or paid subscription?"

---

### Phase 4: Product Feedback (10 minutes)

**Goals:**
- Collect specific UX feedback
- Identify bugs or issues
- Understand feature needs

**Questions:**

1. **UX Feedback**
   - "What did you think of the interface? Easy to use?"
   - "Was anything confusing?"
   - "What did you like most?"

2. **Feature Requests**
   - "What features would make this essential for you?"
   - "What would make you tell a friend about this?"

3. **Objections**
   - "What would prevent you from using this?"
   - "What concerns do you have?"

---

### Phase 5: Closing (5 minutes)

**Goals:**
- Summarize key insights
- Get commitment (beta test, referral)
- Thank participant

**Script:**
```
"Thank you so much for your time! This has been really helpful.

Based on what you said, I heard:
- [Key insight 1]
- [Key insight 2]
- [Key insight 3]

Is that accurate? Did I miss anything important?

We're launching a beta soon. Would you be interested in being an early tester? We'd give you free premium access for 3 months.

Also, do you know anyone else who struggles with meal planning who might want to try this?"
```

**Next Steps:**
- Send follow-up email with thank you
- Include beta signup link
- Offer to share results when ready

## Post-Interview Analysis

### Within 24 Hours
1. **Transcribe** (if recorded)
2. **Summarize** key insights in template
3. **Tag** by theme (pain point, feature, pricing, etc.)
4. **Share** with team

### Weekly Review
1. **Synthesize** patterns across interviews
2. **Update** product roadmap based on insights
3. **Identify** top 3 pain points
4. **Prioritize** feature requests

### After 10 Interviews
1. **Create** interview insights report
2. **Present** findings to team
3. **Update** product positioning based on feedback
4. **Iterate** on product based on validated insights

## Common Interview Pitfalls

### ❌ Avoid Leading Questions
- ❌ "Don't you find meal planning frustrating?"
- ✅ "How do you feel about meal planning?"

### ❌ Avoid Hypotheticals
- ❌ "Would you use this?"
- ✅ "Walk me through trying this right now."

### ❌ Avoid Selling
- ❌ "This solves all your problems, right?"
- ✅ "Does this solve the problem we discussed?"

### ❌ Avoid Yes/No Questions
- ❌ "Do you meal plan?"
- ✅ "Tell me about your meal planning process."
